## FINDING 1 -- the letter's question is ANSWERED (MEASURED): the delta
## carries a nonzero, launch-bound, state-dependent additive residual R

counter_ns = [encoder start, encoder end] = T_dispatch + R(grid shape,
state) + epsilon, with |epsilon| bounded by the tick granularity
(FINDING 4). R is MEASURED at four launch shapes, each cold and warm
(medians over n=12 brackets; min/max beside; raw pairs in the logs):

  shape            cold median (min..max)      warm median (min..max)
  one-group        9750.0 / 9792.0 (8958..13416, 39583*)   4208.5 (4083..5542, 32292*)
  zero-groups      1875.0 / 1833.0 (1791..2834)            979.5 / 1000.0 (875..1083)
  arms-grid        20604.5 (19750..24167)                  7833.5 (7583..8292, 67833*)
  arms-grid-tgm    20791.5 (19834..21042, 150167*)         8000.0 (7750..8167)

  (one-group and zero-groups appear in both v3 and v4; both medians are
  listed where they differ. * = single named outliers, FINDING 6.)

At the fold arms' launch shape (3907 x 256): R ~= 7.83-8.00 us WARM,
20.6-20.8 us COLD. The residual is NOT a constant and NOT separable
into sampling vs encoder-boundary vs driver cost through this bracket;
it decomposes only into measured bounds (warm): an encoded-dispatch
floor of ~0.98-1.00 us (zero-groups), a per-grid-launch add-on of
~3.21 us (one-group over zero-groups), and a launch-scale add-on of
~3.63 us (arms-grid over one-group). v1's spin series (recorded rounds,
n=20 per level, medians):

  iters=0 4500.0 | 1024 33645.5 | 4096 121333.5 | 16384 472145.5 |
  65536 1875583.0 | 262144 7505062.5 | 1048576 30016729.5 |
  4194304 120105708.5 | 16777216 480578708.0

  low-band least squares (iters <= 65536): delta = F + c*iters,
  F = 4417.7 ns, c = 28.5515 ns/iter; the top levels bend slightly
  (per-iter cost creeps 28.55 -> 28.64), so no single line fits all

## FINDING 3 -- the two arms' launch shapes differ by less than the
## spread (bounded, NOT zero-claimed)

The baseline arm's run_bracket shape (arms-grid-tgm: threadgroup memory
WG*4 = 1024 bytes) minus the subgroup arm's (arms-grid: 0) = 187.0 ns
cold / 166.5 ns warm (medians). Distributions overlap (warm: arms-grid
7583..8292, tgm 7750..8167). The two arms' additive floors therefore
agree to within ~0.3 us at this launch shape; this is a BOUND, not a
claim of equality, and no differential correction is designed here.

## FINDING 4 -- GPUTimestamp quantizes at 125/3 ns (MEASURED)

Over EVERY raw ts value in all four logs -- 776 values spanning cold
AND warm states -- the maximum deviation from the 125/3 (41.667) ns
lattice is 0.651 ns, and the deviations take exactly three values
{0.041, 0.326, 0.651} ns: the truncation signature of a 125/3-ns tick
printed as integer ns (integer truncation of k*125/3 leaves fractional
parts {0, 1/3, 2/3}). The 381 fresh deltas (stale/unwritten pairs
excluded) deviate <= 0.667 ns from the same lattice, 0 above 1.0 ns --
a FINER tick would scatter deltas across the 125/3 lattice and none
deviate. The counter's effective resolution is therefore 125/3 ns =
41.667 ns (24 MHz-class tick), state-independent (the same lattice
holds cold and warm). The integer gcd over (ts - ts_min) is 1 ns
(presentation rounding), not the tick.

CLOCK DOMAIN stays UNMEASURED, named with its reason: whether 1 tick
equals 1/24 MHz of wall-clock ns needs a reference OUTSIDE this
instrument, and rule 3 bars merging this channel's magnitudes with any
host wall-clock or the command-buffer GPUStartTime->GPUEndTime bracket
(the three all say "ns" and are three instruments). The lattice being
state-stable is a within-channel fact only.

## FINDING 5 -- a bracket with no dispatch is INVALID (route fact,
## re-confirmed with counts)

Across v1+v2: 30 "empty" brackets (compute encoder, no dispatch
encoded) -- 2 resolved 0/0 (the sample buffer was never written), 28
resolved the PREVIOUS bracket's pair byte-identically (stale). The
bracket REQUIRES a dispatch inside the encoder to write its samples;
the counters harness's one-dispatch-per-encoder contract already
guarantees this, and the fact explains the harness's untimed read-back
dispatches using a plain unsampled encoder.
