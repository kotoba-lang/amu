DECISION: the claim rules this campaign's records already practiced ad
hoc are now the protocol for sign and magnitude claims in this evidence
family (the gpu-subgroup-reduce measurement records). The regime term is
a claim-admissibility problem, not a footnote: it was named at every one

W = 64/128/256/512/1024), and at the closing cell it quantified as a

THE PROTOCOL (four rules):

1. Regime classification is published, not hidden. Every measurement
   record publishes each run's baseline per-run median gpu-ns per
   operator and the regime clusters its session observed (the discrete
   clock levels; the harness reads no GPU clock, so clusters are the
   observed baseline-median levels, named by their gpu-ns ranges). A
   magnitude claim whose record does not publish them is inadmissible.

2. Sign claims are regime-invariant or regime-scoped, and only a
   regime-invariant sign is a selection basis. "Regime-invariant": every
   per-run ratio of every observed regime lies on the same side of 1.0
   (the flip-set cells' 18/18 slower). A sign that follows the regime
   (the W = 32 straddle) is published regime-scoped and cannot choose a
   variant -- the standard that kept the LABELED BOOTSTRAP GLSL runs
   verdict-free and the W = 32 answer decision-locked, now stated once
   for every cell.

3. Magnitude claims are regime-matched. A magnitude comparison across
   sessions or cells is drawn within matched regime strata. When the
   regime mixes differ, the claim names the split ("magnitude splits by
   clock regime") and attributes the deviation to session mixing -- it
   is NOT published as a failure to reproduce. The reproduction
   vocabulary is exactly three phrases: "reproduces in sign strictly"
   (all per-run ratios hold the historical sign), "magnitude reproduces
   within <delta>" (matched regime mixes), "magnitude splits by clock
   regime" (mixed).

4. Nothing here measures anything. No :measured? flag, no workgroup row
   (ADR-0355/0358), no flip gate and no selection answer moves. The
   protocol changes what a record may CLAIM, not what any row answers.
