THE DECISION: YES -- cross-cutting-only is a legal refuted shape.

Vocabulary, decided precisely: identity refutes on partition
disagreement alone (any divergent run pair). The disagreement's SHAPE
is the refinement relation the machinery already publishes (equal /
one refines the other / cross-cutting) -- it classifies the refutation,
never licenses or denies it. A refuted record is REFINEMENT-STRUCTURED
when it has at least one strict refinement edge, CROSSING-ONLY when it
has none (at least one pair cross-cuts -- the record refutes -- and no
pair stands in strict refinement); a crossing-only record whose every
operator pair cross-cuts is CROSS-CUTTING-ONLY, this record's shape.

Grounds (design reasoning over the cited texts and numbers; no new
measurement):

1. The definition conditions refutation on a divergent pair and
   publishes refinement as "the disagreement's shape" (ADR-0360
   amendment 2026-10-23 (f); cross_operator_level_identity's own
   docstring). Cross-cutting is already a first-class member of the
   published vocabulary ("{o1} and {o2} cross-cut (neither partition
   refines the other)" is rendered prose today). Nothing in the
   definition makes refinement a legality condition.
2. The pin's comment framed the break as a SURFACING EVENT ("would
   fail this pin and surface instead of hiding"), not an impossibility
   claim. The pin asserted an observed corpus tally (19/19 on
   2026-10-23) as a drift detector; the drift it detected is real data
   (ground 3), and a drift detector whose trigger is real data has
   done its job -- it must be re-stated, not the data denied.
3. The counterexample is real, machine-reproduced above from committed
   summary.md: 5/15 divergent pairs, refinements empty, all three
   pairs cross-cutting.
