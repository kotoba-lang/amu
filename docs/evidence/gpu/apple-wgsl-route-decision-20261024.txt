THE DECISION (ADR-0360's 2026-10-24 amendment, decided in three parts;
the full text lives in the ADR):

YES -- the WGSL route (:webgpu / :wgsl-v1, one route two names, the
ADR-0359 decision-3 idiom) carries Apple's :subgroup-reduce row under
the SAME rules 3 + 6 gate as the Vulkan and MSL routes.

  Part 1 -- the row extends with no new gate: the WGSL arm of the
    shared subgroup-size-parametric emission already exists (emission
    first, selection after), the fold's width is queried at runtime and
    never baked in (rule 6's S | W gate), and no new fact, key or floor
    is invented. Apple reaches this route by NAMING it -- ADR-0355's
    Apple vendor-default target stays :msl-v1 -- exactly as NVIDIA
    reaches it by naming it.
  Part 2 -- the measured-device narrowing RIDES ALONG. A descriptor
    naming the measured device ("m1 max", uB5G idiom) answers :baseline
    on the WGSL route too: the refutation is of the FOLD on that device
    (slower than :baseline on the two arms that ran -- GLSL via
    MoltenVK, MSL natively), and the refuted variant is not selected on
    the device that refuted it on any route that spells the same fold.
    The WGSL arm on that device is UNMEASURED; the conservative answer
    is the measured one. This is a DECISION, not a measurement claim:
    the narrowing's :measured-cell records stay MSL-scoped (an MSL
    measurement never flips a WGSL cell), so every WGSL answer -- the
    narrowed :baseline included -- keeps :measured? false. REOPEN
    CONDITION: a WGSL-arm measurement of the narrowed device is new
    evidence and may revisit the narrowing's route scope.
  Part 3 -- rationale, design fact only, no speed claim: WebGPU-on-Apple
    is a deployed surface (WebKit ships WebGPU; on macOS the browser's
    WebGPU runs over Metal), so a dispatch there previously answered
    :baseline even where the device reports the fold's facts; and the
    AMD row's runtime-width answer covers Apple's fixed SIMD-group width
    a fortiori -- 32 is just the reported S.
